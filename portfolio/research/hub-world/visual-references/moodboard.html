3D desk hub: reference board

The warmth of Life Is Strange, the atmosphere of Stray, and the object-to-world mechanics of The Room. These are supplied reference choices, not claims about the user's personal gaming history.

Warmth and personal storytelling

Life Is Strange · Max's dorm desk

Window light, warm wood and belongings that explain a person. Our robot, ball, camera and development object should feel owned and used.

Compare later: material warmth, purposeful props and clear space around each selectable object.

Screenshot source: TechTudo

Cinematic atmosphere

Stray · Clementine's apartment

Amber practical light, cooler fill and tactile surroundings make the work area feel part of a larger place.

Compare later: light hierarchy and depth while all four portal silhouettes stay readable. Reduce clutter and effect cost.

Screenshot source: Gamepressure

Object-to-world interaction

The Room: Old Sins · Attic dollhouse

A single object contains smaller spaces. Approach a recognizable desk object, reveal its category world, then return to the hub.

Compare later: hub framing, selected-object approach, world reveal and restored camera. A still does not prove motion.

Screenshot source: MiniReview

Where our concept stands

  • The desk asset provides geometry; room lighting, personal props and the four-object composition are still unimplemented.
  • Proposed route: one R3F canvas → CameraControls approach → one active mini world → return with camera and focus restored.
  • Use existing licensed models and scene code. These games supply reference principles, not production models, textures or audio.
  • Future visual reviews must show real browser captures beside these references and explain differences in framing, light, materials, clutter and discoverability.

Current implementation comparison: Not tested. No hub scene was built for this board.
